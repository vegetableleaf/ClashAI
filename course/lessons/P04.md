## 1. Freeze the miniature rules

An **episode** is one complete simulated run from initial conditions to an ending or chosen limit. Our board is one lane, with an enemy tower at y=0 and movement toward decreasing y. One toy tick represents one toy second. Start at tick zero, five elixir, and thirty tower health. Use the eight named base Icebow cards and their lesson costs. The first four form the hand; the remaining four form the queue. A None action means wait. This syntax uses Python's existing missing-value object as an explicit action label; it is not an unknown match outcome.

```python
deck=['Knight','Skeletons','Ice Wizard','X-Bow','Tesla','Rocket','Tornado','The Log']
print(deck[:4])
print(deck[4:])
print(len(set(deck)))
```

**Output:** `['Knight', 'Skeletons', 'Ice Wizard', 'X-Bow']`, `['Tesla', 'Rocket', 'Tornado', 'The Log']`, and `8`.

The deck order is fixed, so the seed affects combat only. That makes two sources of variation easy to separate. Real opening hands, levels, evolution cycles, and abilities are outside this contract. You will mention those limitations in the final write-up rather than quietly implying they are implemented.

## 2. Keep a real four-card cycle

A **queue** processes entries in first-in-first-out order. On a legal play, remove the queue's front card, put it into the played hand slot, and append the played card to the queue's end. The hand and queue each remain length four. An illegal action leaves both unchanged and spends no elixir. list.index finds the played slot; pop(0) removes and returns the front queue entry. These operations are enough for eight cards, even though larger queues may benefit from other data structures. Each helper returns copies so the supplied state remains evidence of the previous instant.

```python
hand=['Knight','Skeletons','Ice Wizard','X-Bow']
queue=['Tesla','Rocket','Tornado','The Log']
slot=hand.index('Skeletons')
hand[slot]=queue.pop(0)
queue.append('Skeletons')
print(hand)
print(queue)
```

**Output:** `['Knight', 'Tesla', 'Ice Wizard', 'X-Bow']` and `['Rocket', 'Tornado', 'The Log', 'Skeletons']`.

Only the played slot changes. The example mutates local lists for clarity; the milestone copies its inputs first. Preserve the combined multiset of eight card names after every successful cycle. This catches accidental duplication or disappearance that a length-only test would miss.

## 3. Play cards with explicit simplifications

A legal card must be known, in hand, and affordable at the start of the tick. Spend its cost and cycle the hand. Knight, Skeletons, and Ice Wizard create one toy unit at y=8 with health six, speed two tiles per tick, and damage two. X-Bow and Tesla create stationary toy units at y=2 with the same health and damage. Rocket, Tornado, and The Log each immediately remove one tower health in this project. These spell rules are intentionally unrealistic placeholders for distinct game effects; no claim about real damage, pulling, targeting, or splash follows from them.

```python
unit={'card':'Knight','y':8,'hp':6,'speed':2,'damage':2}
building={'card':'Tesla','y':2,'hp':6,'speed':0,'damage':2}
print(unit['y']-unit['speed'])
print(building['y']-building['speed'])
```

**Output:** `6` and `2`.

The shared unit representation keeps the project small enough to inspect. A realistic implementation would separate multiple troop bodies, building lifetime, spell geometry, and target rules. Here the objective is a consistent transition pipeline with card costs and cycling, not a miniature reverse-engineering claim.

## 4. Tower shooting and movement

A living tower shoots the nearest unit once per tick, with damage randomly one or two. Nearest means smallest y; ties use the earlier list position. Then remove dead units. Surviving units move forward by their speed, clamped at zero. Units at y<=2 deal their damage to the tower. Health cannot fall below zero. This order is deliberate: a unit killed by the tower cannot move or attack that tick. random.Random(seed) supplies a local stream. Keep one generator for the whole replay, passing it to fight and step rather than reseeding inside either helper.

```python
units=[{'y':4,'hp':3},{'y':2,'hp':5}]
index=min(range(len(units)),key=lambda i:units[i]['y'])
print(index)
units[index]['hp']-=2
print(units[index]['hp'])
```

**Output:** `1` and `3`.

min with key chooses the index whose unit has the smallest y. B10 introduced lambda; here it supplies the comparison value. Tower shots have unlimited toy range, another explicit simplification. A realistic range check would be a separate change with new tests rather than an undocumented tweak.

## 5. Integrate and record the trace

After the action and combat, regenerate exactly one elixir capped at ten, then increment tick. This regeneration is a toy rule, not the 2.8-second live approximation from D01. A **terminal state** ends the episode; here tower_hp=0 is terminal. Stepping a terminal state returns an unchanged deep copy and consumes no random draw. replay(seed,actions) creates one generator, starts the state, and returns the initial snapshot plus one snapshot for every requested action. After terminal, repeated snapshots remain identical. This fixed trace length makes the contract easy to test.

```python
from copy import deepcopy
state={'tick':0,'tower_hp':0}
def terminal_step(s):
    return deepcopy(s)
next_state=terminal_step(state)
print(next_state==state)
print(next_state is state)
```

**Output:** `True` and `False`.

Equal values need not be the same mutable object. The is operator tests identity, whereas == tests equality. Keeping them separate protects history. The final milestone compares full traces from identical seeds and scripts, checks invariants across all frames, and proves the battle can actually progress to a destroyed tower.

## Common mistakes

- Replacing the whole hand instead of the played slot.
- Regenerating before checking affordability when the contract says spend first.
- Allowing a dead unit to attack after the tower removes it.
- Reinitializing the RNG every tick.
- Comparing only final HP instead of every replay snapshot.
- Claiming the toy spell rules or tick rate match Clash Royale.
