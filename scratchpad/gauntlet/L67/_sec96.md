**D. What this establishes, and what it does not.** Established (a): **the S2 slope (+1.6 pp/doubling, measured on OUR crawl across v3/v4/v5) does not transfer to this corpus** -- the same instrument, the same deck, one change (603 more replays), no cell gain. Established (a): the same data DOES improve card choice and calibration, so the replays are not junk and the conversion is not broken (a broken conversion would degrade both heads, not improve one). NOT established (b): *why* the two heads split. Three candidates, none tested: (i) skill/tier -- these are Ranked max-level games, not the pro/ladder-top tier our crawl targets, and placement is the tier-sensitive skill while card choice is more deck-determined; (ii) the placement head is at a *representation* ceiling, not a data ceiling (consistent with §5cs.48 phase-not-resolution and §5cs.94's saturating rollout, both of which said the objective/inputs, not the sample count, are what bind); (iii) engine-drive noise on foreign replays (78.4% crowns match) adds label noise that hurts the spatially precise head more than the categorical one. The cheapest discriminator for (i) vs (ii) is a **held-out split by source**: score v6lat separately on the HF-sourced val rows and the crawl-sourced val rows -- if placement agreement is lower on HF rows specifically, it is tier; if equal, it is the head.
