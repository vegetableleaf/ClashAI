### §5cs.85 -- L66d (2026-09-07 05:4x-06:3x UTC): **the VM drives replays 5.0x faster than this box on identical work -- 2.48 s/match vs 12.52 s -- so S3 compute is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper than §5cs.55 assumed. Separately, YouTube's bot detection stopped the sweep at 11/100, and the icebow hit rate is now 1/20**

**A. Engine throughput, apples to apples (a).** Same 24 tags, same `--record-every 20 --record-plays --determinism-every 10`, same 17 that drive successfully (the other 7 are the known evo-E-barb class), and **both sides produced 17 files with a median of 240 recorded frames** -- verified, because identical output is what makes the timings comparable.

| | s/match median | batch wall | matches/hour/slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| this box (Windows, WHPX, 64% CPU) | **12.52** | 254 s | 288 |
| clashbot-s3 (Linux, KVM, 1 slot) | **2.48** | 48.3 s | 1,452 |

**5.0x.** On cores that benchmark *slower* (0.79x integer, 0.56x float, §5cs.80 E). Contention is not the explanation: the local box read **12.52 s/match at 64% CPU now**, which is *worse* than the 10.71 s median measured while training was saturating it, so the local figure is not depressed by load. The remaining candidate is the hypervisor -- Windows WHPX versus Linux KVM for Android emulation -- which is untested as a cause and would need a same-host A/B to prove.

**B. A near-miss worth recording (c).** The first VM run reported **1.43 s/match** and I nearly had a 7.5x headline. It was measuring different work: I had omitted the recording flags, so the drives produced `frames: 0`. The check that caught it was reading the output files rather than the timing line -- `frames: 0` vs the local corpus's 240. **A throughput number is meaningless until you confirm both sides produced the same artifact**, which is the §7 "never compare two instruments" rule wearing a new hat.

**C. What this does to S3 sizing (b, arithmetic on a measured rate).** At 2.48 s/match on a single slot, 100,000 driven matches costs **69 VM-hours (~$27)**; the sandbox supports up to 8 slots per AVD, and if slots scale even sub-linearly the same work is single-digit dollars. Against the §5cs.55 estimate of ~400 slot-hours, **compute has stopped being the S3 constraint** -- on the local box that same 100k would be 347 hours. Multi-slot scaling is **untested** and is the next thing to measure; nothing here says 8 slots give 8x.

**D. The sweep was stopped by YouTube, not by us (a).** Of 100 videos, **11 downloaded and 88 failed** with `Sign in to confirm you're not a bot`, plus 2 timeouts. Four-way parallel fetching tripped bot detection partway through. The documented workaround is `--cookies-from-browser`, i.e. authenticating as the owner's Google account -- which is a **materially different risk from anonymous fetching**: it attaches the activity to a personal account and makes account-level enforcement possible. The owner acknowledged the ToS point when the operation was anonymous; this is a new fact, so it goes back to them rather than being assumed covered. The untested cheaper option is simply to fetch slowly and serially.

**E. Hit rate, updated (a).** The 10 sweep videos that did download all scored **0.487-0.584**, i.e. none reach the 0.60 threshold. Combined with the paired pilot set: **1 icebow in 20 videos = 5.0%**, Wilson 95% CI **0.9-23.6%**. Against 441.6 channel hours that is **22 h of icebow footage, band 4-104 h**, versus the **78-139 h** one corpus doubling needs (§5cs.80 C). The point estimate is now well short and the upper bound barely reaches the requirement. **The direction of evidence has moved against video mining as a scaling lever** -- 20 videos is still a small sample, but it is no longer consistent with "dozens if not hundreds" of icebow videos on this channel.
