Icebow Round 2

Round 1 found one thing that reliably makes the model stronger in the simulator: the F1 training method, trained for longer. Nine other single changes came out level with their twins, or worse. The live ladder has not yet confirmed that the simulator's gains are real, because live samples have been far too small to see them. Round 2 first settles that question. It then spends most pod time on what worked (longer, better-seeded training on the newer simulator) and on making the simulator's opponents punish what human 12–14k players punish.

The simulator sees big gains; live cannot see them yet

Trophies went from 11,816 (10-10) to about 12,300 (10-15 evening), with a best read of 12,427. That is roughly +100 a day of play. The chart compares each model's simulator win rate on the live-mirrored test (960 games) with its live record.

Round 1

What round 1 taught us

  • (a)F1 is the one robust gain. GAE credit plus critic warm-up beat r1 by +6.8 / +9.6 / +9.7 on three seeds. Training seed 74 to 250 updates added +7.7 on the live-mirrored test. Longer training was mixed on the other seeds (+1.0 / −0.8 at 150 updates).
  • (a)Nine single changes at 65 updates came out level with their twins or worse. Tower and solvency rewards, F3, F4, the critic context heads C2 (−6.5) and F2, the low-elixir pro anchor F6′, λ 0.98, live-like opponent mix, and the posture head C1. A 960-game paired test only resolves differences of about 4 points or more.
  • (a)Single seeds mislead. The live-like mix went +4.8 on one seed and −2.6 on the next; C1 went +4.8 then +0.4 / −0.2. Any training claim needs three seeds.
  • (a)Play/wait thresholds are model-specific. Defensive thresholds gave +3.3 on u65 and −7.4 on u250. Every new checkpoint needs its own quick threshold check before going live.
  • (a)Our simulator bots match human strength but not human style. F1 u65 wins 52.9% against them vs 51.4% live, but they play about 40% faster and react about twice as often. Slowing them to human pace makes them far too easy (F1 wins 70–78%).
  • (c)"Always focus offensive X-Bows on one tower" is worse in the simulator: −3.6 (95% range −6.7 to −0.7). Pros still split lanes far less than our bot (34% of matches vs 65–82%). Whether human defenders punish splitting more than our bots do is (b) untested.
Round 2

Proposed experiments

Each experiment changes one thing against a matched control on the same engine, uses three seeds for any training claim, and states a decision rule before it runs. Pods end their own job chain with a self-stop.

Gate · does the simulator measure live strength?

G1

Live calibration run

needs your live time
Why
Every training decision rests on the simulator. Its biggest predicted gap is u250 over r1 at +15.9 points. If live shows nothing like that, more simulator training is wasted until the simulator is fixed.
Test
About 150 matches each for u250 and r1, alternating blocks of 5 at the same trophy level, on the main account with the current options. 95% range ±11 points; about an 80% chance to confirm if the full simulator gap is real.
Decide
Live gap ≥ +8: trust the simulator for selection and push training (T1, T2). Live gap near 0: pause training work and put the pods on fidelity (S1, O1, O2).
Cost
~18 h of live play, no pods. About 20–25 extra losses on r1 if the simulator is right.

O1

Human-paced opponents that are still strong

(b)
Why
Humans think slower but choose better. Our bots make up for weaker choices with instant reactions, so the model may learn to beat reflexes rather than decisions.
Test
RL-train opponent policies under human tempo limits (a decision every second, plus a 1 s reaction delay), starting from the pro-imitation model and playing against u250. Target: style gap ≤ 0.6 (today 0.97) and F1 u65 wins within ±3 points of its live rate. Then train icebow against them (3 seeds, one change vs F1).
Decide
Use them as the evaluation opponent only if both targets are met; adopt them in training only if the 3-seed run beats its twins on the live-mirrored test.
Cost
1 pod about 10 h for the opponents, then 3 pods about 4 h.
O2

Heavy-deck exploiters in the league

(b)
Why
Your earlier idea. More Mega Knight exposure through ordinary opponents did nothing for beatdown (+4.2 / −5.5 / +2.1 across seeds). Opponents trained specifically to beat our model with Golem, Lava, PEKKA bridge spam, Mega Knight and Royal Giant should find the holes ordinary opponents miss.
Test
Train one exploiter per archetype against frozen u250 until it wins clearly. Add them to the training league for 3 seeds and compare with F1 twins on the beatdown slice of the live-mirrored test.
Decide
Keep if the beatdown slice improves on all three seeds without losing the rest.
Cost
1 pod about 12 h for the exploiters, then 3 pods about 4 h.

Training · spend compute on what worked

T1

Long F1 runs, three seeds, 500 updates

(a) direction measured
Why
Longer training is the only lever with a measured gain beyond F1 itself (u65 → u250 +7.7). It was seed-dependent, so pick the best checkpoint per seed rather than trusting one curve.
Test
Seeds 74/75/76 on 0.1.35, scored on the live-mirrored test at 150, 250, 350 and 500 updates.
Decide
Wire the best checkpoint only if it beats u250 on the same engine with the 95% range above zero and is not worse on the standard test; then T4 before it plays live.
Cost
3 pods about 24 h each.
T2

Pro anchor on every decision

(b)
Why
The original imitation-then-RL design kept the model close to pro play, but the code anchors each run to its own starting point. F6′ anchored only the play/wait choice at low elixir, and was null. RL may drift into habits pros avoid: both-lane X-Bows 82% vs pros 34%, and quick cheap defences.
Test
A KL leash to the pro-imitation model on gate, card and cell; 3 seeds × 65 updates vs F1 twins, plus habit counters (lane split, cheap-defence share, Rocket vs X-Bow when behind).
Decide
Keep if the live-mirrored test does not drop and at least two habit counters move toward pros; then add it to T1's recipe.
Cost
3 pods about 4 h.
