# PRO_EAGERNESS: is the low-elixir eagerness imitation fit or distribution shift? (L74 econ2, option 1, 2026-10-14)

Script `pro_eagerness.py` (run on pod 12037: `/workspace/results/PRO_EAGER2`, about 4 min on GPU, nice 19, 2-3 threads, no
cpu.lock). Numbers are in `pro_eagerness.json`. Every bucket for every model is in `PRO_EAGERNESS_tables.md`, which is
regenerated by `python pro_eagerness.py report`. The per-row arrays (`rows.npz`, 17 MB) stay on the pod only.

## What was measured

- **Pro rows**: `eval_gen.val_rows` (split 1) of `gen_dataset_v32_fv5.npz`, 349,264 rows from 1,459 replays. **deck** =
  the 38,317 rows from 284 replays whose 8 cards equal the bot's deck (X-Bow, Tesla, Ice Wizard, Knight, Rocket, Log, Tornado,
  Skeletons). Because the gate depends on the deck, **deck** is the primary comparison and **all** is secondary.
- **Models (gate p = sigmoid of the gate head)**: r1 plain `newsim_base_main_u0060.pt`; ADAPT `wk_adapt_main_latest.pt`
  (55c8f49e); `gen_v1_s0/gen_s0.pt` (fv1, loads; 1 card id unmapped); and `gen_v32_s0/gen_s0.pt`, the pure imitation model
  on this same dataset (**imit_v32**, copied to the pod for this run). Lineage: r1 was initialised from ADAPT, and ADAPT
  from rseries_r3c_u0030_barrel. Whether imit_v32 is an ancestor of r3c is not verified.
- **Own states**: all 2,400 distill games (`/workspace/results/DISTILL_ADAPT/gen`, 936,813 learner decisions, D3 stack). The
  p of the plain ADAPT checkpoint equals the p recorded by the deployed grafted teacher (max |diff| 2.4e-6), so the graft does
  not touch the gate.
- **Sampling correction**: on the SIM side, every comparison with pros uses the **pro-style sampled subset**
  (`train_distill.select`: accepted plays + 1 wait row per 2 s, none within 1 s before a play; 303,650 rows). Pro label
  rates and bot label rates therefore come from the same sampling, and p is compared within a bucket.
- **Deployed rule**: p > tau, with tau = .35 / .45 / .55 for 1x / 2x / OT.
- **CIs**: 95% Poisson bootstrap over matches (pro: the replay id `rep`, both sides together; SIM: the game), B = 1000.
  SIM-minus-pro intervals are differences of two independent bootstraps.

## Definitions

These are read from each row's own observation. Board frame: me at the bottom, token y >= 0.5 is my half.

- **e**: own elixir, sc[3] x 10. It is exact, and on play rows it is the pre-act frame (elixir before the play), the same as
  a SIM decision row (checked in `dataset.build_replay`).
- **phase**: from the tick. 1x is tick < 2400, 2x is tick < 3600, OT is after that.
- **value**: `decision_options.body_value(cls)`, which is card cost / bodies at full hp, the same value econ2 uses for pushes.
  Spells and ability markers count 0.
- **vm**: enemy value on my half, all bodies, the same as econ2's vm.
- **vt**: enemy **troop** value on their half (buildings excluded).
- **rec**: the summed elixir of the opponent's non-spell plays in `opp_past` (their last 3 public plays) that are <= 15 s old.
- **UNDER_ATTACK**: vm >= 3.
- **APPROACH**: vm < 1 and vt >= 3 and rec >= 3. A push is committed with recently deployed units, and none of them are on my
  half yet. fv5 rows carry no unit age or velocity, so recency comes from `opp_past`.
- **QUIET**: vm < 0.5 and vt < 1 (their buildings ignored).
- **OTHER**: everything else.

**Proxy check against econ2** (SIM decision series; push start = econ2's rule, vm >= 7 after >= 80 ticks below):

- 47% of APPROACH decisions are followed by a push start within 15 s. The rate is 11% for QUIET, 26% for OTHER and 27% for
  UNDER_ATTACK.
- Of the decisions in the 10 s before a push start, 47% are APPROACH, 36% UNDER_ATTACK, 12% OTHER and 5% QUIET.
- So econ2's commit -> push-start window is mostly APPROACH plus UNDER_ATTACK.

## Verdict

**1. Imitation fit is not the cause. (c) contradicted.**
- On the pros' own icebow states, the pure imitation model is calibrated at low elixir. Mean p is within the CI of the label
  in 1x APPROACH and UNDER_ATTACK.
- At the deployed taus it plays **less** often than pros in APPROACH (5.8 vs 8.3) and about as often under attack
  (19.8 vs 17.6, CI includes 0).
- QUIET is under-played by every model.
- gen_v1 is even less eager (tables file).

**2. The deployed models are too eager on the pros' own states, but because of RL drift, not imitation. (a) measured.**
- The eagerness grows from imit_v32 to ADAPT to r1. In 1x deck rows, the excess of the p > tau share over the pro label:

  | context, 1x deck | imit_v32 | ADAPT | r1 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | UNDER_ATTACK 0-4 | +2.2 | +7.2 | +11.8 |
  | APPROACH 0-4 | -2.5 | +2.5 | +3.3 |

- r1 at 0-2 elixir: 7.7% vs 1.7% in APPROACH and 18.5% vs 4.4% under attack.
- Pooled over all phases, APPROACH is under-played by all models (higher taus in 2x/OT), so this part is a 1x problem.

**3. Distribution shift is real and is the larger part in the approach window. (a) measured.**
- On its own SIM states, ADAPT fires more often than on pro states with the same elixir and context:
  - APPROACH 0-4, 1x: 16.4% vs 10.7%. That is +5.7 [3.0, 8.4], or +3.3 [0.5, 5.6] after reweighting.
  - APPROACH 0-4, all phases: +9.2 [7.5, 10.7], or +6.3 after reweighting.
- About 40% of the raw shift comes from the bot's approach states holding bigger pushes (vt 6.45 vs 5.49). The rest is the
  model acting differently in the same elixir/vm/vt/phase cell.
- Under attack, there is no shift in p (+0.8 [-2.3, 4.0] after reweighting).

**4. Shares, for ADAPT in the econ2-relevant cell (APPROACH 0-4, 1x).**

| part | share of the bot's +6.7 pp over pros (15.0 vs 8.3) |
|---|---|
| on-pro-state eagerness (RL drift, fit) | about 1/3 (+2.5) |
| distribution shift (own states) | about 2/3 or more (+5.7: +2.4 state mix, +3.3 same-cell) |
| decision layer | -1.4 |

Under attack at low elixir, the shares come out differently:
- The causes are RL drift (fit +7.2 for ADAPT and +11.8 for r1, in 1x) and, over all phases, the decision layer: +4.6, the
  `--tau-threatened 0.2` rule.
- 24% of the bot's low-elixir UNDER_ATTACK plays are `tau_threat` plays with p <= tau.
- Distribution shift is about 0 here.
- The bot also spends 1.7x more of its time in low-elixir under-attack states, which is a consequence of earlier spending.

**Implication. (b) plausible, untested.**
- Supervised recalibration toward the pro labels can remove the on-pro-state part. That part is larger for r1 than for
  ADAPT, especially under attack. Concretely: re-anchor the gate at low elixir to imit_v32's level, or use a KL leash to the
  imitation gate rather than to the RL init.
- It cannot remove the own-state shift, which is the larger part in APPROACH. That part needs an on-policy signal: RL that
  is punished for approach spending, or a decision-layer change.
- Separately: tau_threatened 0.2 adds under-attack plays at low elixir. Whether those plays are harmful is untested.

## Caveats

- The own-state half is **ADAPT, not r1**, and **SIM, not live**. r1's own-state shift is untested (b). Measuring it needs
  distill rows from an r1 teacher (gen_distill.py, about 75 min on a pod).
- Live-only decision rules (afford 23 / extrapolate 24, ...) are not in SIM D3, and the live-only third of econ2's gap is
  not addressed here.
- "Shift" means the model behaves differently on the bot's states. It does not prove the model is wrong there: no pro label
  exists for those states. Reweighting controls only elixir, vm, vt and phase. Hand, tower hp and opponent cards can still
  differ within a cell.
- The deck subset is small (284 replays; n = 233 to 1,482 per 1x low-elixir cell), so its CIs are wide.
  - The all-deck rows agree on under attack and on the shift (APPROACH 0-4, 1x: +11.3 raw, +7.7 after reweighting).
  - They disagree on APPROACH fit: there, r1's p > tau is below the label (-1.0 [-1.7, -0.3]). The approach-fit excess is
    specific to the bot's deck.
- The context labels are a proxy. They were validated on the SIM decision series only, not on pro rows.
- p > tau per row is not a plays-per-second rate. It is comparable between pro and SIM only because both use the same
  row sampling.
