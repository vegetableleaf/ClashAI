1. WHAT IT IS
   The MODEL may decide again while one of its own plays is tapped-but-unconfirmed (live) / decided-but-not-landed (SIM), instead of
   the hard lock.  Rules (live and SIM share them; the verdict is e1_eval.follow_up_verdict, the same function the follow-up taps use):
    * newest frame only; SIM: on the 2-tick reader-frame grid from the first frame after the first tap returned
    * at most FOLLOW_MAX_OUT = 2 outstanding; no decision at the cap; the frame that shows the last landing never decides
    * the pending play is a play already made: its bodies / spell on the look-ahead board (extrapolate.pending_board), my hand + next as
      e1_eval.pending_hand shows them, my elixir minus its cost, newest past play, own_effects at its landing tick
    * the pending card's hand position is masked (cost inf live, hand mask SIM): never re-picked, never re-tapped
    * elixir = mine (afford_ticks / look-ahead ahead) minus every unconfirmed tap, in the mask AND in the final shared verdict
    * gate / card / cell are the model's own; the decision options apply unchanged; lethal Rocket does not fire while a play is pending;
      the hazard decoder's step = real ticks since the previous decision (0 right after a play)
    * a refused first play changes nothing: the second decision stands on its own (live test: second play still tapped, own outcome)
    * one knob, default 0: --pipeline-tau-delta X adds X to the gate threshold ONLY while a play is pending
   Code: pipeline/e1_eval.py (Match + SelfPlaySide = the search_s0 benchmark path; SelfPlayMatch.due lands each play at its own tick),
   pipeline/live_gen.py (pending view), live_gen_v2.py (tau delta, lethal), decision_options.py (per-row tau_delta / hazard step),
   extrapolate.pending_board (ported from the failed v2 branch), search_s0.py (--pipeline-decisions / --pipeline-tau-delta /
   --record-plays), live_play.py (--pipeline-decisions / --pipeline-tau-delta).

2. THE TWO EARLIER ATTEMPTS (HANDOFF "PIPELINED SECOND PLAYS", branch worktree-agent-a613a3951c4d5dae9, L74/pipeline_plays) AND WHY THIS ONE
   v1 (--pipeline-plays): SIM v3 960 paired OFF 628 vs ON 487 (114 better / 255 worse, p < 1e-4); with the pending bodies on the board 534.
   v2: OFF 634 / pending view 570 (121/185, p .0003) / + hazard decoding while pending 584 (144/194, p .0076).
   Measured causes: (a) consecutive plays <= 20 ticks .195 / .086 / .077 vs pros .044 (the pending lock had been a RATE LIMITER on the
   threshold gate); (b) the second CARD: P(Tornado | Tornado affordable) at second plays .50 vs pros .16 (bot 30 ticks after a play .09-.12);
   elixir at play 7.14 -> 6.79, at big-push start 5.13 -> 4.81; (c) diagnostics: the model on PRO second-play rows picks Tornado .111 (pros
   .162), through the pending view .111-.121 -> neither model nor view over-picks; the SIM .45-.50 is SIM-specific (look-ahead with the
   pending effect, SIM board states, or a gate x card selection effect); no single view part fixes it (largest: no look-ahead .353;
   no elixir deduction .216); the decisive check (fork the SIM, land the pending card for real, re-score) was never run.
   Cadence arm (decide_every 10 vs 4): push-start elixir 4.50 vs 4.00 -> any decision-rate increase overspends.
   How this design answers them (and what it does NOT):
    1. rate limiter: the window is still bounded (<= 2 outstanding; no decision at the cap; the landing frame never decides) AND the
       gate sees the spent elixir + the play in its past, so p collapses right after a play (offline replay below: median gate p .46 ->
       .09 on the state right after the model's own play).  The remaining risk is a gate that fires on p > tau within the window:
       --pipeline-tau-delta is the one calibrated knob; the A/B arms pd1 / pd2 test it.  This is not a hardcoded second-play rule (default 0).
    2. the card: the view is the complete one from v2 (bodies + projectile/area + hand/next + elixir + past + own_effects) PLUS the mask
       and a verdict that cannot be bypassed; acceptance is judged on the Tornado share of second plays against the pros' (.119) --
       it is a MEASURED bar, not assumed fixed.  The decisive "land it for real" check is a pod diagnostic (section 3, step 4).
    3. overspending: elixir at play and at big-push start are in the bar (>= reference - 0.3).
    4. byte-identical default, and the SIM runs the SAME rules (shared verdict, shared pending_hand, SelfPlaySide benchmark path) so the
       A/B is fair; v1/v2 ran only the SIM on SelfPlaySide and the live path was a separate implementation.
   Differences that remain between SIM and live: SIM decides on the frame grid only INSIDE a window (otherwise every 10 ticks), live
   decides every frame; SIM has no hand-tap loss (live lost 1 board tap in 12 pairs); SIM B lands exactly delay ticks after its decision.
