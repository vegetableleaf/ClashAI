The whole path, screenshot to click

1. Capture — a plain program finds the game window and grabs the pixels (pygetwindow + mss). No AI.
2. Reading — two things happen to that image. Template matching (plain pixel comparison) decides which screen is this and which cards are in hand; the vision AI finds and names the units on the board. Both write into the SAME numeric form the simulator produces.
3. Deciding — the playing AI receives exactly that: numbers. It cannot tell a real match from a simulated one, and it answers the same way in both — (card, cell).
4. Acting — also no AI. ActionSpace.decode turns (card, cell) into four coordinates in 0..1, WindowCapture.to_screen maps those onto real pixels using the window position, and Controller.play_card clicks twice: the card in the tray, then the spot on the board.

Training data for the vision AI. Drag a box around each unit on the board, pick what it is, save. That is the whole job -- the detector learns to predict the boxes you drew. Frames are collected automatically while train-rl runs.

This project trains two separate networks. They have different jobs, different training commands and different files, and the playing AI is half blind without the vision one. Each has one model you actually use; the extra files below it are that same model at different stages, not competing AIs.
